# Code of conduct

This covers everywhere OwnBase is worked on in public: issues, pull requests,
discussions, reviews and the project's other spaces. It also covers anyone who
speaks for the project somewhere else.

## What is expected

- Be kind and patient, including with people who are new, unsure or wrong.
- Talk about the work, not the person. Criticise code, ideas and decisions, and
  say what would make them better.
- Assume good faith. If something reads badly, ask what was meant before you
  answer it.
- Accept a no, or a decision you disagree with, once the reasons are given.
- Credit other people's work, and say so when you got something wrong.

## What is not accepted

- Insults, slurs, or jokes at someone's expense, including about their gender,
  sexuality, disability, appearance, body, age, race, ethnicity, religion,
  nationality or level of experience.
- Harassment, in public or private: unwanted contact, following someone across
  sites, intimidation, or sexual language and images.
- Publishing someone's private information, such as their address, real name
  or messages, without their permission.
- Repeatedly derailing a thread, or pushing on after you have been asked to
  stop.
- Anything else a reasonable person would see as unwelcome in a professional
  setting.

## Reporting

Email <nikhil.rathore@bug0.ai>. Say what happened, where (links help), and who
was involved. Only the maintainer reads these reports, and your name is not
shared with anyone without your permission. A report about the maintainer can
go to GitHub instead, through **Report abuse** on their profile or the content.

A security vulnerability is not a conduct report. Send it privately as
[SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) explains.

## What happens next

OwnBase has one maintainer, so there is no fixed response time, but every
report is read and answered. Depending on what happened, the response can be:

1. A private note saying what was wrong and what to do instead.
2. A public warning, with the comments in question edited, hidden or removed.
3. A time-limited ban from the project's spaces.
4. A permanent ban.

The person reported gets a chance to give their side, unless doing so would put
the person who reported them at risk.
