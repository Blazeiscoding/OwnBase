Moving in from Slack

import-slack makes a new workspace from the export Slack makes. In Slack, a workspace owner or admin opens Workspace settings → Import and export data → Export. Every plan exports public channels; private channels and direct messages come only in the exports Slack's Business+ and Enterprise plans can apply for. The import brings over whatever the export holds.

ownbase-server import-slack --from "./Acme Slack export Jan 1 2024 - Nov 13 2026.zip" \
  --data ./data --owner alice --name "Acme"

--data must be a new or empty folder; a workspace is never imported into one that already has history. --owner is a Slack username, as the export's users.json has it. That person becomes the owner and is given a temporary password, printed once at the end, which leads straight to choosing their own. Everyone else comes in deactivated, with no password anyone knows; the owner brings each back from People, which gives them a temporary password to pass on. Admins in Slack are admins here. Slack's guests come in as members, since a guest here is someone without an account. Then start the server on the folder as usual:

ownbase-server --data ./data

What comes over: people, with their names and time zones; public and private channels with their topics, descriptions, members and archived state; direct and group messages; and every message with its time, author, edits, thread, reactions and pins. Mentions of people and channels point at them here, and emoji names such as :smiley: become the emoji, as Slack showed them. Exports from 2015 and 2016, which write a few fields differently, import the same way. A message's id is made from the time Slack gave it, so history pages back in the order it happened, and search finds imported words. History comes in read: nobody opens the workspace to thousands of unread messages.

What does not: join and leave notices and notices of topic or name changes, which this app does not post; reactions with a custom emoji; and text past 12,000 characters, which is cut there. Posts by bots and integrations stay, each under a bot account named after the bot. Someone who wrote in a channel but is not in users.json, as happens with people from another organisation in a shared channel, comes in deactivated under the name their messages give. The summary at the end counts everything left out, with the reason.

Files need a choice. Slack's export holds links to files, not the files. By default each message keeps a file's name as a line of text. With --fetch-files the import fetches each one through its link (Slack puts a token on it) and keeps it as an upload, up to 100 MB each. A link stops working once the file, its channel or the Slack workspace is deleted, so fetch before closing Slack down. A file Slack will not give keeps its name and is counted.

The import reads a zip of up to 64 GB and 2 million files, up to 256 MB in one JSON file. About seven minutes per million messages on a recent desktop, more with --fetch-files. If anything fails, everything it made is removed and the error names the file and field it could not read.
